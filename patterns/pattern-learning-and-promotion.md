# Pattern Learning & Promotion

The Patterns layer may improve from repeated project experience, external evidence, standards, incidents, and recurring failure knowledge, but repeated agent behavior is not automatically reusable knowledge.

> Repeated behavior is not reusable knowledge until evidence, scope, and transferability justify promotion.

## Pattern Candidates

New reusable insights should first become Pattern Candidates rather than accepted Patterns.

A candidate may arise from:

- recurring implementation experience,
- repeated project decisions,
- recurring failure modes,
- production incidents,
- external standards or research,
- strong domain-independent evidence,
- or material lessons from existing Patterns.

A candidate remains proposed reusable knowledge until it passes deliberate review and acceptance.

## Promotion criteria

Promotion should evaluate whether the candidate has:

- a clear recurring problem space,
- meaningful reuse beyond one project,
- sufficient evidence or trustworthy experience,
- known applicability and anti-applicability boundaries,
- useful forces and trade-offs,
- transferability across relevant contexts,
- and a correct ownership layer.

No fixed project-count threshold is required. Strong external evidence may justify promotion without repeated internal use, while many repeated examples may still be too project-specific or weakly evidenced.

## Project knowledge is not automatically reusable knowledge

Project Brain knowledge must not be copied upward merely because it worked once or appears important.

Promotion requires separating project-specific decisions from genuinely reusable reasoning.

A successful local architecture may remain a project decision rather than becoming a Pattern if its value depends materially on local constraints.

## Agent learning boundary

Agents may:

- identify recurring knowledge,
- propose Pattern Candidates,
- collect evidence,
- challenge existing Pattern guidance,
- suggest deprecation, retirement, or ownership changes.

Agents may not silently:

- create accepted Patterns,
- promote candidates into canonical reusable knowledge,
- rewrite Pattern governance,
- or convert runtime inference into accepted framework truth.

Acceptance remains governed through normal framework authority and review.

## Avoiding self-reinforcing habits

Repeated use of the same solution is not evidence by itself that the solution is good.

The Pattern system must distinguish repetition from validation. Agent defaults, copied implementations, convenience, or historical habit can reproduce mediocre or harmful solutions across projects.

Promotion should therefore examine outcomes, alternatives, failure evidence, and transferability rather than counting repetition alone.

## Existing Pattern improvement

New evidence may justify:

- clarifying a Pattern,
- changing applicability or anti-applicability,
- adding failure modes,
- revising recommendations or trade-offs,
- deprecating or retiring guidance,
- or proposing that knowledge belongs in another framework layer.

If an insight proves universal rather than problem-specific, it may be a Core candidate. If it proves domain-specific, it may belong in a Profile instead of Patterns.

Such movement must be explicit and reviewed rather than silently changing layer ownership.

## Promotion principle

> **Pattern learning should convert trustworthy, transferable evidence into reusable intelligence without turning repetition, agent preference, or project-specific success into false best practice.**

> **Agents may propose learning; they may not silently promote it into canonical framework truth.**
