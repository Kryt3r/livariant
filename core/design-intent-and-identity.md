# Design Intent & Identity

Design work begins with identity and intent, not with components, trends, or default interface patterns.

> **Design begins with identity and intent, not components.**

The purpose of this standard is to ensure that product design remains deliberate, recognizable, usable, and coherent instead of collapsing into statistically common visual defaults.

## Design intent

Before meaningful visual design begins, establish what the experience should communicate and how it should feel.

Relevant questions include:

- What should users feel when they encounter this product or surface?
- What should receive attention first?
- What should feel trustworthy, powerful, playful, calm, mysterious, premium, direct, or restrained?
- Which expectations of the target audience should be met?
- Which expectations should be intentionally challenged?
- Which visual or experiential qualities must explicitly be avoided?

Functional requirements alone are insufficient to establish design direction.

## Identity and anti-identity

A design direction should define both desired and undesired qualities.

Example:

```text
Desired:
- mysterious
- premium
- alive
- exploratory
- deep
- coherent with the fictional world

Avoid:
- generic mobile gacha appearance
- casino-like reward presentation
- stock fantasy template aesthetics
- overloaded MMORPG interface density
- generic AI-generated landing-page patterns
```

The anti-identity prevents drift toward visually competent but product-inappropriate defaults.

## Recognition-based design

The framework must support users who reason about design primarily by recognition and comparison rather than by internally visualizing a finished result.

> **Recognition-based design must be supported; imagination must never be a prerequisite.**

A user must not be required to describe a fully formed visual concept before meaningful design work can begin.

Instead, the process should be able to externalize design through:

- references,
- alternative directions,
- wireframes,
- mockups,
- prototypes,
- comparative descriptions,
- annotated screenshots,
- and iterative critique.

The user may respond with observations such as:

- direction A feels right,
- direction B is too sterile,
- navigation from C works better,
- density from D is preferable,
- or a particular element should be combined with another direction.

This is a first-class design workflow, not a fallback.

## Reference intelligence

References are evidence and learning material, not templates to copy.

> **References are analyzed for transferable principles, not copied as solutions.**

When analyzing a reference, extract relevant characteristics such as:

- composition,
- hierarchy,
- information density,
- typography,
- spacing,
- shape language,
- navigation,
- imagery,
- motion,
- interaction patterns,
- environmental integration,
- and emotional effect.

The objective is to identify why a reference works and which properties are transferable to the project.

A project should not conclude merely that it "likes website X". It should aim to understand which underlying decisions create that preference.

## Design memory

Repeated design feedback should become reusable project intelligence when it has durable value.

Examples may include:

```text
Accepted tendencies:
- asymmetrical composition
- restrained border radius
- environmental UI integration
- strong depth and hierarchy

Rejected tendencies:
- generic card grids without structural need
- excessive glassmorphism
- decorative gradients without purpose
- oversized empty hero sections
```

These records are contextual design knowledge, not immutable taste laws.

They may be scoped to a project, product area, feature family, or design phase and may evolve with new evidence.

## Design quality dimensions

Visual preference alone is not sufficient to approve a design.

Meaningful design work should be evaluated across at least:

### Desirability
Does the result fit the intended identity and emotional direction?

### Usability
Can users understand, navigate, and operate it effectively?

### Coherence
Does it fit the rest of the product and preserve a recognizable system identity?

Accessibility, technical feasibility, operational constraints, and performance may add further required dimensions depending on context.

Constructive dissent applies to design decisions. A visually appealing result may still require challenge when it damages usability, accessibility, product clarity, or system coherence.

## Divergence before convergence

Meaningful design decisions should not default to the first plausible solution.

> **Meaningful design work should diverge before it converges.**

Alternative design directions must differ in meaningful structural or experiential ways rather than superficial styling.

Bad divergence:

```text
A — blue buttons
B — purple buttons
C — green buttons
```

Meaningful divergence:

```text
A — WORLD-FIRST
The interface recedes and the environment carries most of the experience.

B — SYSTEM-FIRST
Information, progression, and control dominate the composition.

C — HYBRID
The world forms the stage while systems are embedded into it.
```

The purpose of divergence is to reveal choices, tradeoffs, and possible identities before committing to implementation.

## Purpose before pattern

Common patterns are not automatically good choices.

> **Patterns must justify their presence through purpose, not popularity.**

Cards, gradients, glass effects, large hero sections, pills, dashboards, sidebars, and other familiar patterns are not prohibited.

They become weak decisions when their only justification is familiarity, trend, or model-default behavior.

For recurring visual patterns, the designer or agent should be able to explain what problem the pattern solves in the current context.

## Design Critic perspective

The design process should include a critical perspective separate from pure generation.

The Designer asks:

> How could this work and look?

The Design Critic asks:

> Why does it look and behave this way, and what is this decision actually accomplishing?

The critic should actively search for generic or unjustified design behavior such as:

- unnecessary cardification,
- decorative effects without semantic purpose,
- interchangeable illustrations,
- weak hierarchy,
- default SaaS layouts applied to unrelated products,
- excessive uniformity,
- motion without interaction value,
- oversized headings that dominate without reason,
- and repeated patterns whose only justification is that they are common.

The goal is not stylistic contrarianism. It is deliberate design.

## Design System vs. Design Intelligence

A traditional design system may preserve:

- tokens,
- typography,
- spacing,
- components,
- interaction primitives,
- and visual rules.

Design Intelligence additionally preserves:

- why decisions were made,
- which directions were rejected,
- which qualities define product identity,
- which anti-patterns were discovered,
- which references proved useful,
- which user problems a pattern solves,
- and which design experiments succeeded or failed.

Design state and design rationale are complementary but distinct forms of project knowledge.

## Design decisions become project knowledge

> **Design decisions become project knowledge.**

When design work produces durable insight, it should be consolidated through the framework's knowledge lifecycle.

Do not document every visual experiment. Preserve only what future design work can use to reduce rediscovery, maintain identity, or avoid repeated failure.

## Baseline design loop

The baseline design process is:

**Understand → Identity → References → Diverge → Compare → Critique → Converge → Prototype → Test → Refine → Consolidate**

The depth of each phase is proportional to the importance, risk, and longevity of the design decision.

Trivial visual changes must remain trivial.

Meaningful identity-shaping work should not be reduced to prompt-to-interface generation.

## Core principles

> **Design begins with identity and intent, not components.**

> **Recognition-based design must be supported; imagination must never be a prerequisite.**

> **References are analyzed for transferable principles, not copied as solutions.**

> **Meaningful design work should diverge before it converges.**

> **Patterns must justify their presence through purpose, not popularity.**

> **Design decisions become project knowledge.**
